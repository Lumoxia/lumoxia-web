# Requirements: Terms of Service page for The Royal Game of Ur

## Goal

Lumoxia has a Privacy Policy, a Support page and an Age Suitability page for *The Royal Game of Ur*, but no Terms of Service. Add one page, at `/royal-game-of-ur/terms/`, that serves the iOS app and the Android app alike. It must be robust: accurate to what the app really does, enforceable against a player anywhere in the world, and fair enough that consumer-protection law in the UK, the EU and the United States does not strike its clauses down.

Primary jurisdictions are the UK, the EU and the United States. "Enforceable globally" is met by drafting rather than by listing countries: governing law and courts stated plainly, mandatory local consumer rights preserved instead of fought, a severability clause, and an English-prevails clause. A term that a court would void for consumers (a blanket liability waiver, a forced arbitration clause, a class-action waiver, a one-sided change right) is left out, because it would endanger the clauses around it and the studio's standing with the stores.

The page is built with the existing pipeline: a template in `templates/`, strings in `i18n/strings/*.json`, `python3 scripts/build.py`, and the generated pages committed with the sources. Generated pages are never edited by hand.

This is drafted as product copy by an engineer with AI assistance. It is not legal advice, and the owner has it reviewed by a qualified lawyer before the page is linked from a store listing (see Decision 4).

## Facts the terms must be consistent with

Source: the English Privacy, Age Suitability and Support pages (`i18n/strings/en.json`), the README, and the live site.

- The app is a two-player dice and strategy board game (single player against the computer, or two players on one device). There is no online play, chat, user-generated content, accounts, ads, analytics or tracking. It works offline.
- One optional, one-time in-app purchase unlocks every board and stone colour. Boards and colours can also be unlocked free by playing. There is no subscription. Apple (App Store) or Google (Google Play) takes the payment, keeps the record and handles refunds.
- The app has links to other websites and services (Lumoxia on GitHub, YouTube, Spotify, ElevenLabs credit, the Support and Privacy pages).
- The app can store settings, statistics and progress on the device, and the phone's own backup may copy them to the user's Google Account or iCloud. Lumoxia receives and can read none of it.
- Contact for everything is `rgur-support@lumoxia.art`. The site's voice is a solo studio: no "we", no team language, no employers or job titles.
- The game, its rules, the board and the dice have ancient roots and are not owned by anyone; the app's code, artwork, sound design and arrangements, and the Lumoxia name and logo, are Lumoxia's. Some content is by others and credited in the app's Rules panel (for example the sound effects credited to ElevenLabs).
- The site is in fifteen languages and CI requires every string file to carry the same keys, tags and tokens, with no em or en dashes.

## Acceptance criteria

### The page

1. **One page for both platforms.** `/royal-game-of-ur/terms/` exists in all fifteen languages (`/fr/royal-game-of-ur/terms/` to `/ko/royal-game-of-ur/terms/`), built from a new `templates/terms.html`, titled "Terms of Service", with a "Last updated" line giving the date in each language's own format. The text says it covers the iOS and Android versions of the app, and it does not describe the two platforms separately except where store rules differ.
2. **Findable.** The page is linked from the footer of every page (home, Privacy, Support, Age Suitability, Terms), and from the nav links of the app pages next to Privacy and Support. The Privacy page's sentence about the app's Rules panel is not changed, because the app's Rules panel does not link to the Terms. The page is in `sitemap.xml` with `hreflang` alternates for all fifteen languages, like the Privacy page. A reader on any page can reach it in one click.
3. **Readable.** It opens with a short plain-language summary ("In short: ...") in bold, as the Privacy page does, followed by numbered or titled sections, short paragraphs, no legalese for its own sake. Sections that the stores or a regulator require to be conspicuous (warranty disclaimer, limit of liability) are set in a visually distinct style the existing CSS already supports (`<strong>`), not hidden in a paragraph.

### Content: what the terms say

4. **Agreement and acceptance.** The terms are an agreement between the player and Lumoxia.Art ("Lumoxia"), the publisher named under criterion 16. Downloading, installing or using the app accepts them; a player who does not agree must not use the app and can uninstall it. They are in addition to the store's own terms (Apple Media Services Terms and Conditions, Google Play Terms of Service), which win where they cannot be varied.
5. **Licence.** Lumoxia grants a personal, non-exclusive, non-transferable, revocable, worldwide licence to install and use the app on devices the player owns or controls, for personal, non-commercial use, as permitted by the store's usage rules. The app is licensed, not sold. Lumoxia keeps all rights not granted.
6. **What the player may not do.** Copy, modify, reverse-engineer, decompile or extract the app's assets (code, artwork, audio), except where law gives that right regardless; sell, rent, sublicense or redistribute the app; remove notices; use it unlawfully; use the Lumoxia name or logo without written permission; scrape or load the website in a way that harms it. The rules of the ancient game itself stay free for everyone to play and teach; the page says so, so that the restriction cannot be read as owning the game.
7. **Intellectual property.** The app's code, artwork, board designs, sound design, arrangements and text, and the Lumoxia name and logo, belong to Lumoxia or its licensors. Third-party material (credited in the app's Rules panel, and trademarks of Apple, Google and the streaming services) belongs to its owners. Nothing in the terms transfers ownership.
8. **The optional purchase.** The terms state: one optional, one-time purchase unlocks every board and stone colour; the price is shown by the store in local currency with taxes where applicable; payment, billing, refunds and cancellation are handled by Apple or Google under their rules, not by Lumoxia; the unlock is tied to the player's store account and is restored through the store's restore/refresh mechanism on a new device; a purchase is not a subscription and does not renew; Lumoxia cannot refund through the app because it receives no payment details; boards and colours earned free by playing are not purchases. If the store later changes how unlocks work (for example, store removal of the app), Lumoxia keeps the unlock working for existing buyers on a best-efforts basis and says so honestly rather than promising it forever.
9. **Consumer rights that cannot be waived.** The page states in plain words that nothing in the terms limits rights the player has by law and cannot lawfully be limited, and names the main ones without pretending to be exhaustive: in the UK, the Consumer Rights Act 2015 (digital content must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described, with a right to repair, replacement or price reduction); in the EU, the Digital Content Directive (EU) 2019/770 as implemented where the player lives, and the right of withdrawal from a digital-content contract, which the store presents at purchase and which the player may lose once the unlock is delivered with their consent; in the United States, state consumer-protection law. These rights are exercised against the store where the store is the seller, and the page says that too.
10. **Warranty disclaimer.** To the extent the law allows, the app is provided "as is" and "as available", without implied warranties of merchantability, fitness for a particular purpose or non-infringement, and without a promise that it is uninterrupted or error-free. The disclaimer is expressly "subject to the player's statutory rights" so it stays valid for consumers, and is set conspicuously for US enforceability. The page also states the required store clause: in the event of any failure of the app to conform to a warranty, the player may notify Apple, which refunds the purchase price if any, and, to the maximum extent permitted by law, Apple has no other warranty obligation.
11. **Limitation of liability.** Lumoxia's liability is limited to the extent the law allows, with the carve-outs that make the clause enforceable in the UK and EU: nothing excludes or limits liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, for fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation, for gross negligence or wilful misconduct, for anything the law says cannot be excluded, or, for consumers, for a breach of the statutory rights in criterion 9. Beyond those, no liability for indirect or consequential loss, lost data from a device or backup failure, or loss caused by third-party stores or services; and the overall cap for any claim is the amount the player paid Lumoxia through the store for the app in the twelve months before the claim, or, if nothing was paid, a nominal fixed sum stated on the page. The cap is worded so that a court striking one part leaves the rest standing.
12. **Third-party services and links.** The app and site link to services Lumoxia does not control; those services have their own terms and privacy policies; Lumoxia is not responsible for them.
13. **Privacy.** The terms point to the Privacy Policy and say it is not part of the contract but describes how data is handled; the terms make no claim that conflicts with it (no data collected, backups made by the platform).
14. **Updates and changes to the app.** Lumoxia may update or change the app, and may stop supporting an old version, but will not remove paid content a player has already bought without a legal reason or a remedy; a change that makes the app materially worse for a paying player is not made unilaterally. The page distinguishes updates (store-delivered) from the terms themselves.
15. **Changes to the terms.** Lumoxia may change the terms for a clear reason (a law, a store rule, a change to the app, a correction), by publishing the new text at the same address with a new "Last updated" date, before the change takes effect for a material change, with a notice period of at least 30 days. A player who does not agree to a material change may stop using the app and, where law allows, has a right to the remedies in criterion 9. Changes never apply retroactively to purchases already made. The old versions of the English text are kept in the repository history and not otherwise published.
16. **Who the publisher is.** The page names the publisher as **Lumoxia.Art** (the name the owner gave, written exactly so), says "Lumoxia" is its short form in the text, and gives `info@lumoxia.art` as the address for notices, legal questions and complaints. The owner has chosen to publish no postal address. The page states this plainly ("notices by email to info@lumoxia.art") and does not invent an address. Known risk, recorded for the owner and the lawyer: Apple's minimum licence terms ask for the developer's name and address, and UK and EU trader-identification rules expect a geographic address, so a store or a regulator may require one in App Store Connect or Play Console trader details even though the page omits it. `rgur-support@lumoxia.art` stays the contact for app support. Giorgio's own name, employers and job titles are never mentioned; the page presents a solo studio.
17. **Governing law and courts.** The governing law is that decided in Decision 1 (recommended: England and Wales). The page adds, as an unconditional exception, that a consumer keeps the protection of the mandatory laws of the country where they live, and may bring a claim in the courts of that country (the rule of EU Regulation 1215/2012 and the UK Consumer Rights Act). There is no arbitration clause and no class-action waiver, because both are unenforceable or risky for consumers in the UK and EU and invite challenge in several US states, and the stores' own terms already cover most disputes. The court clause for business and non-consumer users names the courts of England and Wales (Decision 1).
18. **Informal resolution first.** A player with a complaint is invited, not required, to email the contact address first; Lumoxia replies within a stated period. The page links to the EU Online Dispute Resolution platform only if it is still operating at the time of the build (it was retired in 2025; the page does not link to it) and names the UK-recognised alternative dispute resolution route only if the owner chooses one.
19. **Severability, waiver, entire agreement, assignment.** A part found invalid is cut to the extent necessary and the rest stands; failure to enforce is not a waiver; the terms plus the store terms are the entire agreement; the player cannot transfer the licence; Lumoxia may transfer the terms in a sale of the app and tells players if it does. No third party has rights under the terms, except Apple and Google as third-party beneficiaries as the stores require (criterion 20).
20. **Store-required terms.** The page includes, in plain words, the minimum terms Apple's Licensed Application End User License Agreement requires when a developer supplies its own licence: the agreement is between Lumoxia and the player, not Apple; the scope of the licence is limited by the Apple Media Services Terms; Lumoxia, not Apple, is responsible for maintenance and support; the warranty clause of criterion 10; Lumoxia, not Apple, handles product claims and intellectual-property infringement claims; the player confirms that they are not in a country under a US government embargo or on a US list of restricted parties; the player must follow applicable third-party terms; Apple and its subsidiaries are third-party beneficiaries and may enforce the terms. An equivalent short paragraph covers Google Play (the store's terms apply; Google is not a party and has no support obligation). The Apple text is on the same page, not in a second document, so the App Store Connect "License Agreement" field and the Play listing can both link to one URL.
21. **Export and sanctions.** The player must not use the app where the law of the UK, EU or US prohibits it. One sentence, not a regime.
22. **Termination.** The licence ends if the player breaches the terms or deletes the app; Lumoxia may end it for a serious breach, with the sections that must survive (ownership, disclaimer, liability, law) naming themselves. Termination does not affect a paid unlock the player is entitled to under law.
23. **Age and children.** The page says the app is a family game with no account and no chat; that purchases and the store account belong to an adult or are made with a parent's approval, under the store's own parental controls (as the Age Suitability page says); and that it makes no claim to be a children's service or to be exempt from any law that applies to it. It does not state a minimum age beyond what the stores impose.
24. **Plain, correct, solo voice.** No "we", "our team" or legal filler; "Lumoxia" and "you" as the other pages do; no personal name outside criterion 16; no promises the studio cannot keep (such as "free updates forever", "always works offline on every device").

### Translations and process

25. **All 15 languages change together.** The new keys (`terms.*`, `meta.terms.*`, `footer.terms`, `nav.terms`) are added to `i18n/strings/en.json` and the other fourteen files with the same keys, tags and tokens, no em or en dashes, `_review` left as `unreviewed`. Each non-English page carries a short note, in its own language, that the English text prevails if the translations differ, and the English page says the same in English. The legal-force clauses (governing law, limitation of liability, the licence grant) are translated closely, not paraphrased, and flagged in `i18n/README.md` as the first things a native-speaking reviewer checks.
26. **Existing pages updated, no regressions.** The Privacy, Support, Age Suitability and home pages gain the footer link; the Privacy and Age pages gain a one-line cross-reference ("The terms for using the app are on the Terms of Service page") without changing any privacy fact. `privacy.meta` and `age.meta` dates are not changed, because their facts do not change.
27. **Tests guard the page.** `scripts/test_build.py` gains tests that, for every language: the Terms page is generated at the right path; it is linked from every footer and from the sitemap with all hreflang alternates; it contains the headings and the store-required clauses (the Apple third-party-beneficiary clause and the embargo clause by key); it contains no arbitration or class-action waiver words in the English source; and its Last-updated date is in that language's format. `check_i18n.py` and `test_check.py` pass unchanged. CI "committed pages equal generated pages" is green.
28. **Docs.** `README.md` lists the new page and `i18n/README.md` names the Terms keys; the Store listing note says the Terms URL (`https://lumoxia.art/royal-game-of-ur/terms/`) is the one to enter in App Store Connect (License Agreement, Custom) and in the Play listing, as the Age Suitability URL already is.

## Decisions

1. **Legal identity, governing law and venue (needs the owner).** The page must name who contracts with the player and give a postal address (Apple requires developer name and address; the UK and EU require a trader to identify itself). Recommended: England and Wales law and courts, with the criterion 17 consumer exception, naming the legal name of the sole trader or company behind Lumoxia and a postal address (a business or registered-office address, not a home address if one exists). Alternative: the law of the owner's actual country of residence if it differs, or Delaware or California for a US-facing studio, which is weaker for UK and EU consumers and gains little. Answered 2026-11-20: England and Wales as recommended; the publisher is named "Lumoxia.Art" with no postal address, and notices go to `info@lumoxia.art` (criterion 16 records the resulting store and regulator risk).
2. **No arbitration, no class-action waiver (recommended).** Both leave out. The studio is solo, the app is offline, the price is low and the store already refunds; an arbitration clause would be void against UK and EU consumers, would not bind them, and in the US invites unconscionability challenges while adding cost. Courts plus informal resolution is the robust option. Alternative: a US-only arbitration clause with a class waiver and a 30-day opt-out, which the owner may want if they fear US class actions; it adds length and is the clause most often attacked.
3. **Translations (recommended: all fifteen now, marked unreviewed, English prevails).** CI requires matching keys, so English-only is not possible, and the earlier privacy changes made the same choice. The legal-force clauses are flagged for native review first.
4. **Lawyer review before linking from a store (recommended).** The owner has the final English text reviewed before entering the URL in App Store Connect or Play Console. Alternative: publish the page now as the best-effort terms and link it from the stores only after review. The page is accurate either way, but a bespoke Apple EULA that missed a required term would fall back to Apple's standard EULA, which is why the store-required clauses are mandatory in criterion 20.

## Out of scope

- Any change to what the app does, to the Privacy Policy's facts, or to the Age Suitability page's facts.
- Entering the URL in App Store Connect or Play Console (the owner does that, after review).
- Terms for Patreon, the album or any other Lumoxia product; the page covers *The Royal Game of Ur* and this website only.
- Terms for future features (online play, chat, ads, accounts, subscriptions). If any ships, the terms are updated before it does, as the Age Suitability page already promises.
- A cookie banner or a separate cookie policy (the site sets no cookies).
- Native-speaker or legal review of the translations.
